## 2 Why

Two gains, unequal in size. First, **information**: a profile of a low-level target
tells us what `C1`, `C3a` and `C7` will cost (calling convention, allocation, where the
time goes) months before they are designed. Second, faster evaluation, but only for
work whose loop is spent running FPL (`M2a`). In the work graph no backend lies on the
critical path (0% of draws in the backend spike), so all of this runs in parallel. Its
cost lands on the reviewer, which is the real constraint.

## 3 Shape

A (the CBPV IR) lowers to M (`fpl/asm/common`: a control-flow graph over unbounded
virtual registers, with register roles instead of names). From M, register machines
(RISC-V, Apple ARM, x86) follow by register allocation, and WebAssembly by *stackify*:
virtual registers become locals and the operand stack. B (WebAssembly 1:1) and C
(RISC-V 1:1) are the two concrete instances that M is abstracted from, because an
abstraction designed before two instances guesses.

Precedents: the Categorical Abstract Machine (Cousineau, Curien and Mauny, 1985),
whose instructions are the combinators of a cartesian closed category; Typed Assembly
Language and its stack variant (Morrisett et al., POPL 1998); CompCert, with a shared
machine-independent Mach and one Asm per target, where the simulation proofs of the
passes compose; LLVM and Cranelift, with target descriptions per ISA; LLVM's
WebAssembly backend, which compiles as for a register machine and stackifies at the
end.

The categorical statement to refine: languages are objects, a pass is a morphism
carrying a simulation, and the pipeline is their composite. M is a signature, each ISA
is a model of it, and instruction selection is a morphism of signatures. Correctness is
checked by simulation, not by subtyping, so "inheritance" here means "a model of the
signature".

## 4 Order

B and C depend on nothing in FPL (they mirror external specs), so they can start now
with cold agents, at low risk of slop. For A, the statement comes first and the code
after it is accepted. M waits for B, C and A's statement. D needs A, M and B. E needs
M and C. F (MMIX) is a full track like B and C and starts after them; the common layer
is drafted from B and C and accepted only once F exists, so it is drawn from three
instances without waiting on the third to begin. G follows B and C in review.

## 5 Open questions and their answers

The questions were put to three reviewers (substrate, theory, orchestration); the
answers are recommendations, the author of the design decides.

- **Does the plan reorder D32** (Perceus-C before own assembly)? Yes: WebAssembly comes
  before Perceus-C and the own `.s` backend becomes E. Acceptable, with clang kept as a
  later third route for differential testing, and with §12's three constraints and
  row 80's reclamation test surviving.
- **Which passes are C1's** (row 74)? Everything from A to the residual:
  literal-quotation inlining, closure conversion or defunctionalisation,
  monomorphisation, explicit allocation, Perceus reference-count insertion. Not C1:
  CFG, virtual registers, instruction selection, `stackify`, register allocation. C1
  ends where M begins.
- **Reclamation for the WebAssembly model.** Linear memory with our own reference
  counting, not WasmGC. M carries `alloc`, `retain`, `release`, `reuse` from the start
  (no-ops or bump in D), so the spike changes nothing later. B leaves GC types and
  exceptions out, and Wasm traps count as a root panic (row 43), never as FPL's `Fail`.
- **Is A the residual?** A keeps runtime thunks (`U B`, force). Literal quotations for
  control words are inlined by an early A to A pass; the residual is A after closure
  conversion, not A itself. Level 1 is empty on the default tier (row 38), so A has no
  `Code` type.
- **Is A the syntax of K1?** Yes: A is K1's core, the walker's program after desugar
  with level 1 run away. A drops the surface's strands, saturation and textual stack. It
  keeps capabilities as values, the effect line {`div`, `fail`} (row 26), grades as
  annotations on binders (row 4, counted as in minimal B0), and thunks (row 81).
  Dependent types are not in A; they arrive with K9.
- **A's model** is row 79's Agda statement, a locally indexed adjunction F ⊣ U, so
  `CBPV.agda` and A's statement name the same object.
- **Free additions to M**: a `cost-label` pseudo-operation and per-operation
  observation sets (rows 75 and 77), both no-ops in D.

## 6 Statement for A: the object-level CBPV IR (K1's core)

Levy's call-by-push-value, simply typed, with three additions from FPL: capability
operations, the effect line, and cost labels. Typing is Levy's plus those three rules.
Literals are value constants; primitive words are computation constants of their CBPV
type.

```
Value types        A ::= β | 1 | A × A | 0 | A + A | U B | μD
                         β: the walker's base types and capability types; μD from K5
Computation types  B ::= F A | A → B | ⊤ | B & B
Contexts           Γ ::= · | Γ, x :q A        q a grade (row 4); grade 0 erased by an early A → A pass
Values             V ::= x | c | () | (V, V) | inl V | inr V | thunk M | con V
Computations       M ::= return V | M to x. N | force V | λx. M | V ‘ M
                       | pm V as (x, y). M | pm V as {inl x. M | inr y. N} | pm V as con x. M | absurd V
                       | ⟨M, N⟩ | fst M | snd M
                       | rec x. M        x : U B; puts div on the effect line
                       | op V W          operation op of capability V (row 26)
                       | fail W          puts fail on the effect line
                       | label ℓ. M      cost label: an observation and nothing else
Judgements         Γ ⊢v V : A        Γ ⊢c M : B ! ε,  ε ⊆ {div, fail}

CK machine         ⟨M, K⟩,  K ::= ε | V :: K | (to x. N) :: K | fst :: K | snd :: K | supervisor :: K
⟨V ‘ M, K⟩                  ↦  ⟨M, V :: K⟩                 pushing a value is application
⟨λx. M, V :: K⟩             ↦  ⟨M[V/x], K⟩                 a lambda pops
⟨M to x. N, K⟩              ↦  ⟨M, (to x. N) :: K⟩
⟨return V, (to x. N) :: K⟩  ↦  ⟨N[V/x], K⟩
⟨force (thunk M), K⟩        ↦  ⟨M, K⟩
⟨fst M, K⟩                  ↦  ⟨M, fst :: K⟩;   ⟨⟨M₁, M₂⟩, fst :: K⟩ ↦ ⟨M₁, K⟩   (snd alike)
⟨pm V as …, K⟩              ↦  the matching branch, with V's parts substituted
⟨rec x. M, K⟩               ↦  ⟨M[thunk (rec x. M)/x], K⟩
⟨op V W, K⟩                 ─(op, V, W, r)→  ⟨return r, K⟩   on replay r is read from the log (rows 44, 47)
⟨label ℓ. M, K⟩             ─ℓ→  ⟨M, K⟩
⟨fail W, K⟩                 unwinds K to the nearest supervisor frame (rows 15, 16, 43; frame shape: hole fail-frames)
Terminal: ⟨return V, ε⟩ is a result; ⟨λx. M, ε⟩ is an unsaturated word, which the walker prints as a section.
```

**Words.** A word `f : x: A₁  y: A₂ -- z: C` is a computation of type
`A₂ → A₁ → F C`: its λs pop the top of the data stack first, and several outputs return
a tuple. The data stack is the CK stack's argument frames. Lowering from the walker's
desugared form binds a call's inputs with λ and pushes them above the continuation
frame, which fixed valence (row 14) makes static.

**Observations.** A run's observation is its event trace (capability operations with
their results, and cost labels), ending in a result, in `fail` at the root, or in
divergence. Row 74's per-pass tests compare exactly this.

**Model** (Levy, TAC 2005; verified). A locally indexed adjunction F ⊣ U between a
cartesian category of values ℂ, with distributive coproducts and μD as initial algebras
of row 67's polynomial functors, and a locally ℂ-indexed category of stacks. A value
denotes a ℂ-morphism ⟦Γ⟧ → ⟦A⟧, a computation a ℂ-morphism ⟦Γ⟧ → U⟦B⟧, a stack a
morphism of the stack category. Call-by-value lives in the Kleisli part and
call-by-name in the co-Kleisli part. Concretely, UF is the monad of interaction trees
over the event alphabet (Xia et al., POPL 2020): `Ret` for return, `Vis` for an event,
`Tau` for a silent step. Divergence is then an infinite run of `Tau`s, and a pass is
correct when source and target are equivalent up to `Tau`. This is the object row 79
asks `CBPV.agda` to state, so one file states both.

## 7 Statement for M: languages and passes form a category

An object is a language: programs, states, an initial state per program, a step
relation labelled by events from one shared alphabet (capability events with their
results, cost labels, row 77's observations), terminal states with results, and a
checker for its well-formedness predicates (typing, label-per-loop). A morphism
L → L′ is a pass: a partial compilation function, which may refuse, together with a
forward simulation. Every L-step is matched by zero or more L′-steps emitting the same
events, with a measure against infinite stuttering, and the pass must preserve the
well-formedness predicates. Forward simulations compose, as CompCert composes its
passes, and the identity pass is the identity, so a pipeline is a composite and its
correctness is the composite simulation. With a determinate target, a forward
simulation gives preservation of behaviour, again as in CompCert. Per morphism, rung 2
is row 74's test and rung 1 is the simulation proved.

`asm-common` is a signature of operations over virtual registers with roles, and each
ISA (WebAssembly, RISC-V, MMIX) is a model of it. Instruction selection sends each
operation to a derived operation, an instruction sequence, and the model's semantics of
the selected code must simulate `asm-common`'s, so each model yields a morphism
`asm-common` → ISA. Cost is not part of a morphism: each language has a cost handler
from its steps to a monoid (row 77; CK steps for A, (mems, oops) for MMIX). Because
morphisms preserve label traces, the segments between consecutive labels correspond, so
the per-label difference between adjacent columns is what the pass between them costs.

### 7.1 Sharpened: two kinds of morphism

"Each ISA is a model" hides two kinds of morphism, and the difference decides what can
be computed and what must be measured.

- **Per operation: instruction selection.** A signature morphism σ_I sends each
  operation of `asm-common` to a derived operation of I (an instruction sequence) and
  each register role to a register of I. Models go the other way: the reduct σ_I\*⟦I⟧
  is an `asm-common` model, and "I is a model" means exactly that this reduct simulates
  `asm-common`'s own semantics. This is an institution in Goguen and Burstall's sense
  (JACM 1992), and its satisfaction condition is the useful form: a property proved of
  an `asm-common` program holds of its selected code. The calling convention and the
  reserved registers (x18 on Apple) belong to σ_I, that is, to the model, so H0's ABI is
  a choice of σ_I(call) and σ_I(return) per ISA.
- **Global passes.** Some steps need the whole program and come from no signature
  morphism, and each target family needs exactly one. Register machines have finitely
  many registers, so they need register allocation (liveness) before σ_I. For MMIX,
  with 256 registers and a register stack, that pass is nearly the identity, which is
  why its column measures FPL's passes and not spills. WebAssembly is the mirror case:
  its locals are unbounded, so virtual registers map to locals per operation, but its
  control is structured, so no per-operation σ can express an arbitrary jump, and the
  control-flow graph must be restructured into block/loop/br. Moving single-use values
  onto the operand stack is an optimisation, not required. So WebAssembly is a model of
  the data part of the signature and the target of a pass for its control part.
- **Cost.** Along σ_I, cost is computed, not measured, whenever I's cost model is
  additive per executed instruction: the cost of an operation is the sum of I's costs
  over σ_I(op), a monoid homomorphism. MMIX's (mems, oops) is additive. mmmix's
  pipeline and real hardware are not, and neither are the global passes, whose cost
  (spills, local traffic, restructuring) must be measured. The labels-by-passes table
  therefore has three kinds of column difference: computed (σ), measured (global
  passes), and a change of unit (A → `asm-common`).
- **One predicate to add.** Put reducibility of the control-flow graph in
  `asm-common`'s checker, as label-per-loop is, so the WebAssembly pass never needs node
  splitting. Lowering from A must then produce reducible graphs; mutually recursive join
  points can break that, so the exception gets named rather than found.

## 8 Proposal: MMIX as the insight target (not decided)

The aim is to see *where* FPL's performance comes from, per pass, not how fast it runs.
D's WebAssembly route answers the second question; this answers the first.

**Mechanism.** Every intermediate language already has an evaluator for its per-pass
differential test (row 74). Give each evaluator row 77's cost handler over the same
`cost-label` pseudo-operations (already planned for M, a no-op in D). At A the ticks are
CK-machine steps (row 61's step grade, machine-independent); at the end they are
Knuth's (µ, υ) per MMIX instruction. The profile is then a table, labels by passes, and
the difference between two adjacent columns is what that one morphism cost. Each of
C1's passes becomes visible: literal-quotation inlining as fewer indirect calls
(PUSHGO costs 3υ, PUSHJ 1υ); closure conversion or defunctionalisation as allocations
(mems) and, for defunctionalisation, a tag branch in the good/bad-guess counters;
monomorphisation as removed boxing; explicit allocation as `alloc` made visible; Perceus
as `retain`/`release` header loads and stores, and `reuse` as allocations that
disappear.

**Why MMIX.**

1. 256 general registers with a register stack (PUSHJ/POP renumber the local window),
   so register allocation is nearly trivial and the measured cost is FPL's passes rather
   than spills; RISC-V and stackify mix the two.
2. A third model of M's signature with a different calling convention: information for
   H0, and an abstraction drawn from three instances guesses less than one drawn from
   two.
3. A fixed, published cost table in units of υ, which does not age, for the same reason
   M is `&`-keyed (row 75).
4. Cost is two-dimensional: rC keeps mems and oops apart, so `cost@M` is a product
   semiring, weighted only when asked; row 29's residency term lands on the µ axis.
5. rC counts up while rI counts down: cost grade and fuel on one tick. Fuel stays in
   abstract steps (rows 36, 60) and `cost@M` stays lifted (row 75); merging the units
   would make totality depend on a machine model. Later, mmmix's configurable pipeline
   is a second `M` at the predictable-hardware end of row 76, without building a soft
   core.

**Conditions, with rungs.**

1. Labels survive every pass: make `cost-label` an observation in row 77's sense, so
   row 74's event-sequence test checks label preservation for free (rung 2). Costs per
   label are not preserved; that is what is measured.
2. Every loop in every intermediate language passes through a label (CerCo's soundness
   condition): a well-formedness check in each language's checker (rung 2; rung 1 once
   the simulation proofs exist). The cost table and the evaluators are the same Python
   (rung 2). The `mmix -P` profile is a file on disk (bottom rung), kept as evidence
   that the handler's sum agrees with Knuth's simulator, never as a second source.

**Limits.** The numbers come from a model, not a laptop: good for structure and for
comparing design choices (runtime thunks or not, Perceus on or off); absolute speed
stays with D's wasmtime profile, and where the two profiles diverge the model is wrong,
which is itself a finding. Off the critical path; the cost lands on review, so this
waits behind B and C.

**Answers.**

- *Speed.* It speeds nothing on the critical path, since no backend does; its value is
  information, and that is worth having before `C1`, `H0` and the common layer are
  designed. The cost handler is the cheap half: every intermediate language needs an
  evaluator for row 74's test anyway, labels as observations make label preservation a
  free rung-2 test, and the walker already counts steps for fuel. Fold it into D as the
  optional addition: it adds volume to D's review, not a new slot. F costs a review slot
  and starts after B and C. The 26.05 pin carries mmixware (1.0-unstable-2025-06-30) for
  x86_64-linux and aarch64-darwin.
- *`cost-label` as an observation.* Yes, as a pseudo-operation whose whole meaning is
  its observation (row 77), emitted into the same trace as capability events. Preserving
  that trace is what makes the labels-by-passes table well defined: consecutive labels
  cut every run into segments that correspond across a pass, and the cost per segment is
  what differs. Labels constrain only the order of events, which passes preserve anyway,
  so pure code may move across a label; the table then shows cost moving between labels,
  which is information, not an error. One real cost: a pure labelled loop whose result is
  unused can no longer be deleted, because its labels are events. That is acceptable for
  a measurement pipeline.
- *Label-per-loop* lives in each language's checker, as a well-formedness predicate every
  pass must preserve, not in the syntax or semantics, so a language is the same object
  with or without instrumentation. In the CFG languages (`asm-common`, the ISAs) the
  graph with its labelled nodes removed must be acyclic. In A, loops arise only from
  `rec` and from the bodies that iteration primitives repeat (`fold`, `each`, the scan),
  so each such body begins with a label. The lowering into A inserts them, one per
  definition entry and one per loop body, keyed by the definition's `&` plus an ordinal
  (C1, row 75), so a label means the same thing in every column.
- *Three conditions on the cost handler as D's addition.* (a) Unit boundaries: each
  column is in its own unit (A: CK steps and allocation counts; machine-level languages:
  instructions, or (mems, oops) for MMIX). The difference between two adjacent columns is
  the cost of one pass only within a unit; across the first change of unit (A → the
  common layer) it is a change of measure, and the table marks that boundary. (b) Labels
  are checked as dynamic traces: duplicating code by inlining does not break preservation,
  because what row 74's test compares is the label sequence a run emits; the static
  condition is CerCo's, checked in each language. (c) `mmix -P` output is evidence on
  disk, never a second source.
- *MMIX as a model.* Yes, of the common layer's signature, provided call and return are
  abstract operations whose convention the model supplies (the register-window
  renumbering by PUSHJ and POP) and the cost annotation is a product (mems, oops), not
  one number. It is not a real target: its role is insight, and its good and bad guess
  counters are a small fixed branch-predictor model, a teaching instance for row 75's
  pessimistic bound (branches times penalty).

## 9 Briefs per track

Each track is a brief for a cold agent, refined by its owners before it goes out. All
of them share a preamble.

**Shared preamble.** The public repository is the home. The design record is the
combined draft; refiners inline the rows a cold agent needs. The walker and its REPL are
the executable spec (row 37): every seam is tested against them differentially (rung 2).
Invent nothing the design has not stated: an undecided choice becomes a named hole in
the `HOLES.md` format (*Depends on / Default in force / Closes by / Evidence*). Work on
an `agent/claude/attempt/<name>` branch, commit with the repository's commit script,
keep `make ready` green, and prepare the PR for the owner to merge. When a node closes,
add one line to the log with the date and the number of sessions it took.

### 9.1 A, `cbpv-ir`

*Goal:* the IR every backend pass starts from, FPL's object level (row 79's K1):
simply typed, polarised CBPV. *Statement first* (one page, accepted before code):
syntax with the value/computation split, typing, evaluation as Levy's CK machine, and
the model it is meant to be. Then a lowering from the walker's desugared form and an
evaluator.

Done when: (1) the statement is accepted; (2) the IR evaluator agrees with the walker on
every example that runs and on generated programs that elaborate, compared on the
observation (event trace, then a result, a root failure or divergence); (3) `make ready`
is green; (4) the lowering inserts cost labels, one per definition entry and one per loop
body, keyed by the definition's identifier plus an ordinal, and the checker refuses a loop
that passes through no label; (5) the statement is committed with the rules for `op`,
`fail`, `label` and `rec` and the amendments of row 83; (6) untyped slots, and
overloading that only run time can resolve, make the lowering refuse with a reason (hole
`untyped-slots`); (7) grades are annotations, default ω, unchecked (hole
`grades-unchecked`). Holes to open with their defaults: `effect-placement` (the effect on
the returner), `fail-frames` (one root supervisor; a fail must reach its lexically bound
supervisor). Not now: backends, optimisation, Perceus, dependent types.

### 9.2 B, `wasm-ir`

*Goal:* a Python data model mirroring WebAssembly modules and instructions one to one,
with no FPL in it, plus a printer to WAT, a checker for operand-stack types and a
reference evaluator. Package `fpl/asm/wasm`; it imports only the standard library,
`icontract` and `fpl.asm.*` (an import-linter contract for `fpl.asm` is the gate).

- **Spec, pinned:** WebAssembly Specification Release 3.0 (2026-07-10), the tagged
  `wg-3.0` release, not the living draft.
- **Subset:** i32 and i64 only; function, block and result types; limits, memory, table,
  global types; parametric (`nop`, `unreachable`, `drop`, `select`); control (`block`,
  `loop`, `if`, `br`, `br_if`, `br_table`, `return`, `call`, `call_indirect`,
  `return_call`, `return_call_indirect`); locals and globals; one memory with integer
  load/store including narrow, `memory.size`; integer `const`, unop, binop, testop,
  relop, wrap, extend; modules with types, imports, globals, memory, funcref tables,
  funcs, active data and elem segments, start, exports. The checker follows the spec's
  own algorithm (appendix 7.6.1-7.6.2). Imports and start are modelled, printed and
  checked, not run.
- **Excluded:** floats (NaN nondeterminism), vectors, reference and table instructions,
  `memory.grow` and `table.grow` (nondeterministic), bulk memory and passive segments,
  memory64, multi-memory, extended constant expressions, custom sections, symbolic `$ids`.
  GC types and exceptions are one named hole, `gc-and-exceptions`, default "excluded",
  closed with C2's reclamation. A Wasm trap is a root panic (row 43), never FPL's `Fail`;
  the evaluator reports it as an outcome.
- **Oracles:** wabt 1.0.41 and wasmtime 45.0.2 from the `wasm` layer of the dev shell
  (nixpkgs 26.05, pinned by `flake.lock`). A session-scoped fixture resolves the tools once
  from the worktree's own flake, requires `/nix/store/` paths, checks versions and calls
  the binaries by absolute path; no nix is an error, never a skip. Batch one `.wast` script
  per Hypothesis example through `wast2json` plus `spectest-interp` and through `wasmtime
  wast`. Tail calls need `--enable-tail-call` in every wabt tool. Never `wasmtime run
  --invoke`.
- **No walker seam:** B has no differential against the walker. Its oracles are the two
  validators and the two engines; its evaluator becomes the B side of D's stackify
  differential later.
- **Done when:** (1) every module the valid strategy draws prints to WAT that assembles,
  validates and compiles; (2) the stack-type checker's verdict equals wabt's and
  wasmtime's on generated valid modules and one mutation per invalid kind (mismatch,
  underflow, leftover, label, `br_table` arity, index out of range, immutable global,
  alignment, tail-call result, non-constant initialiser, start type, duplicate export,
  limits), and names exactly the kind; (3) closed generated programs that terminate by
  construction (a fuel global decremented at every function entry and loop head) give the
  same outcome in the reference evaluator, wasmtime and wabt's interpreter: results or trap
  kind, then every exported global; (4) `make ready` is green (100% line and branch
  coverage, mutants killed or allowed with a reason, CrossHair on the contracts).
- Start with the hole `gc-and-exceptions`. Draft PR only; no review request before the
  walker's first slice lands. Not now: lowering from FPL, optimisation, a binary encoder.

### 9.3 C, `riscv-ir`

*Goal:* `fpl/asm/riscv`, FPL-free, mirroring RV64I plus M one to one: every construct a
frozen value, a printer, a parser of what it prints, a checker for the operand forms and
a reference evaluator. RISC-V is the bare-metal target already in §14 (`C3`, `H2`, QEMU
`virt`, row 38) and runs in Linux CI.

- **Spec, pinned:** RISC-V Instruction Set Manual, Volume I (Unprivileged), version
  20250508. Checklist: chapter 35's listing of RV32I, RV64I, RV32M and RV64M minus PAUSE,
  66 mnemonics. Out: PAUSE, Zicsr, Zifencei, C, A, F, D and later extensions, the
  privileged ISA (`H2`'s), pseudo-instructions and aliases, directives and relocation
  operators.
- **Text:** the printer prints what `llvm-objdump -d -M no-aliases -M numeric
  --no-print-imm-hex --mattr=+m` prints for the same bytes (`x10`, not `a0`; decimal
  immediates).
- **Tools** from the `riscv` layer (LLVM 21.1.8, QEMU 10.2.4), resolved by the same kind
  of session fixture. QEMU user mode does not exist for aarch64-darwin; `virt` bare metal
  is the target row 38 names.
- **Observation:** a fixed frame in `tests/`, outside the IR, loads each block's initial
  registers, runs it, writes the registers as bytes to the `virt` UART and exits through
  the test device. Its trap handler reports `mcause` and `mepc` and resumes at the next
  block. A trap is a root panic (row 43), never a value.
- **Done when:** (1) every accepted program assembles and links, QEMU exits 0 within 10 s,
  and the text `llvm-objdump` prints equals the printed text line for line (`llvm-mc`
  silently relaxes an out-of-range branch into two instructions, so "assembles" alone is
  not 1:1); (2) on generated straight-line and forward-branching code the registers QEMU
  writes per block equal the evaluator's (division by zero and overflow per Table 11);
  (3) the checker and `llvm-mc` agree on generated invalid programs; (4) `make ready` is
  green, the oracle tests adding at most 6 s to `make check`.
- Not now: lowering from FPL, register allocation, the privileged ISA, compressed
  instructions, an encoder, GNU as. Draft PR; review only after the walker slice landed.

### 9.4 M, `asm-common`

*Goal:* the machine-independent layer between A and the targets: a control-flow graph of
basic blocks over unbounded virtual registers, with register roles (return value,
argument *i*, stack pointer, link register, scratch) instead of names, and a signature of
operations with B and C as its first two models. *Needs* B, C and A's accepted statement.
Statement first (§7). Done when: (1) the statement is accepted; (2) B and C are given as
models of the signature, with the operations each one lacks named; (3) an evaluator for
M agrees with A's evaluator on lowered examples; (4) `make ready` is green. Not now:
register allocation, selection heuristics, optimisation.

### 9.5 D, `pipeline-wasm`

*Goal:* nanopass (row 74): `cbpv-ir` to `asm-common` through small passes, each with a
declared output language and a differential test (rung 2), then `asm-common` to
WebAssembly by stackify, run in wasmtime. Allocation is a bump heap; reclamation stays a
named hole, so this is a spike toward C2, not C2 (row 80). *Needs* A, M and B.

Done when: (1) every example that runs in the walker prints the same through wasmtime;
(2) the per-pass differential tests pass; (3) one guest profile (`wasmtime
--profile=guest`) of a recursion and a loop is kept as evidence, with a log summary of
where the time goes; (4) the examples are timed against the walker, and `M2a`'s front end
replaces them once it exists; (5) `make ready` is green.

*Optional addition: the per-pass cost handler* (§8, accepted under conditions a–c). It
changes no pass: it is row 77's tick observation with a summing handler. Each language's
evaluator takes the handler over the `cost-label` pseudo-operations (no-ops without it).
At A the ticks are CK steps and allocation counts; after each pass they count what that
language makes explicit (allocations, retain/release, indirect calls). Done when, in
addition: (6) `cost-label` is an observation checked as a dynamic trace: the per-pass
differential tests fail if a pass drops or reorders a label; duplication by inlining is
not a failure; (7) each language's checker refuses a loop that passes through no label;
(8) one labels-by-passes table for the same recursion and loop as the guest profile is
kept as evidence, each column in its own unit, the first change of unit marked as a change
of measure, with a log line on which pass adds what; (9) an external profile (wasmtime's;
`mmix -P` once `mmix-ir` exists) is evidence on disk, never a second source.

### 9.6 E, `pipeline-riscv`

*Goal:* M to RISC-V by instruction selection and the simplest register allocation that
works (stack slots first), run under QEMU in CI; the pipeline's first register machine and
ground for `C3a` (row 38). *Needs* M and C. Apple ARM follows as a third model of M only
when D's profile raises a question that needs real registers on the author's machine; the
author decides that trigger. Check it against Apple's "Writing ARM64 code for Apple
platforms" (x18 reserved, symbols carry a leading underscore, the stack stays 16-byte
aligned). Done when examples agree with the walker under QEMU (and natively once Apple ARM
exists) and `make ready` is green for what CI can run.

### 9.7 F, `mmix-ir` (proposal, not accepted)

*Goal:* a Python data model mirroring an MMIX subset one to one, with no FPL in it, a
printer to MMIXAL, and a cost table (mems, oops) per instruction taken from Knuth's MMIX
documentation, each entry citing its source. Subset: integer arithmetic and logic, loads
and stores, branches including probable branches, `JMP`/`GO`, `PUSHJ`/`PUSHGO`/`POP`,
`TRAP` for `Halt` and `Fputs`; `SAVE`/`UNSAVE` only if a test needs them. Oracles, pinned
in nixpkgs 26.05: mmixware (`mmixal`, `mmix`, `mmmix`). Done when: (1) every program the IR
prints assembles with `mmixal` and runs in `mmix`; (2) on generated straight-line and
looping programs, the cost table summed over the IR's own evaluator equals mmix's reported
mems and oops and its good and bad guesses, with the `mmix -P` output kept as evidence;
(3) `make ready` is green. Not now: lowering from FPL, floating point, mmmix
configurations, interrupts.

### 9.8 G, `aarch64-ir`

*Goal:* `fpl/asm/aarch64`, FPL-free (an import-linter contract forbids `fpl.*` outside
`fpl.asm`), mirroring the A64 base integer instructions one to one: frozen values, a
printer, a parser, an operand checker and a reference evaluator. It is the one target that
runs natively on the author's Mac (Apple silicon) and runs in Linux CI under QEMU user
mode.

- **Spec, pinned:** Arm Architecture Reference Manual for A-profile (DDI 0487), version
  M.d (published 2026-09-29). Subset: base A64 Armv8.0 integer: branches (`B.cond`, `B`,
  `BL`, `BR`, `BLR`, `RET`, `CBZ`, `CBNZ`, `TBZ`, `TBNZ`, `NOP`), loads and stores
  (`LDR`/`STR` with the B, H, SB, SH, SW forms and four addressing modes, `LDUR`/`STUR`,
  `LDP`, `STP`, `LDPSW`), data processing immediate and register including `MADD`,
  `MSUB`, `SMULH`, `UMULH`, `SDIV`, `UDIV`. Semantics from C6.2's pseudocode;
  CONSTRAINED UNPREDICTABLE cases are refused.
- **Out:** `ADRP` (relocation operators differ by object format), literal loads,
  exclusives, acquire/release, atomics, CRC32, pointer authentication, BTI, MTE, CSSC,
  system instructions and registers, SIMD, floating point, SVE, SME, directives.
- **Text:** what `llvm-objdump -d --no-show-raw-insn --no-leading-addr
  --no-print-imm-hex` prints on `-triple=aarch64-linux-gnu`, so aliases print as the
  spec's preferred disassembly (`mov x1, x3`, `cmp`, `lsl`, `mul`, `cset`, `sxtw`, `ret`).
  Not `-M no-aliases`: in LLVM 21.1.8 it still prints `mov`, `asr`, `sxtb`, `bfxil` and
  mis-prints `ldr x1, [x2, x3]` as `ldr x1, [x2, x3, lsl #3]`, which reassembles to other
  bytes. Where LLVM's choice differs from the spec's alias conditions, LLVM's is the text
  and `HOLES.md` names the case.
- **Tools:** LLVM and lld on every platform, `qemu-user` on Linux only; on a Mac the SDK
  (apple-sdk 14.4) comes from the flake's standard environment. The session fixture
  resolves tools once per test session, not once per xdist worker (eight concurrent
  `nix develop` of one flake wait on each other's fetch lock).
- **Observation:** a fixed frame in `tests/`, outside the IR, honouring Apple's ABI notes
  (x18 reserved, x29 a valid frame record, sp 16-byte aligned). Per block it pushes a
  256-byte record, sets NZCV and the initial registers, runs the block, stores the
  registers into the record, and after the last block writes all records to stdout. On
  darwin it is `_main` in a Mach-O (`ld64.lld`, `-lSystem`); on Linux `_start` in a static
  ELF under `qemu-aarch64`. A64 integer code has no traps in this subset (division by zero
  gives 0); a signal is a harness failure, never a value.
- **Done when:** (1) every accepted program assembles and the text `llvm-objdump` prints
  equals the printed text line for line (`llvm-mc` silently rewrites `add x1, x2, #4096`
  to `#1, lsl #12`, `add #-1` to `sub #1`, and `ldr [x2, #3]` to `ldur`); (2) on generated
  straight-line and forward-branching code the registers and NZCV each block reports,
  natively and under QEMU, equal the evaluator's; (3) the checker and `llvm-mc` agree on
  generated invalid programs, and the checker alone refuses the cases `llvm-mc` accepts (a
  load pair with `Rt == Rt2` in pre- and post-indexed `LDP` and pre-indexed `LDPSW`; a pair
  with writeback whose base is a transfer register in post-indexed `LDPSW`); (4) `make
  ready` is green, the oracle tests adding at most 6 s.
- Not now: lowering from FPL, register allocation, calls through the platform ABI,
  `ADRP`, an encoder, GNU as, SIMD/FP. Draft PR; review only after `wasm-ir` and
  `riscv-ir` have been reviewed.
