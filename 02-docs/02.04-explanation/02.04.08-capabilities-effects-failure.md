# 02.04.08 2026-09-27 — Capabilities, effects and failure

C3 of the [substrate](02.04.04-substrate.md) in detail. Authority flows *down* the `..` chain as
capabilities; failure flows *up* the same chain as `/`. The child can only offer, the parent can
only choose among offers: the capability discipline in both directions. The notation is decided in
[syntax rationale](02.04.05-syntax-rationale.md) (paths, `..`, `./`, effect lines); the keys that
loosen it are in [principles and privileges](02.04.07-principles-and-privileges.md).

## 1 Reached by path, used on the stack

Capabilities are values passed lexically. `..` is the enclosing scope, which is the capability that
produced this one; a frame names only what was bound into it or its ancestors, so there is no
ambient dictionary. Paths give access (lexical, non-ambient); the stack gives use. Kitten-style
permission rows were the road not taken: a row is not a value, so it cannot be delegated,
attenuated, sealed or revoked, and it merges input and output into one `IO`.

**Effect-line sugar.** `+in/…` and `+out/…` on the effect line are sugar for a lexically resolved,
linearly threaded capability (Effekt's capability-passing translation; Scala `using` only as a
loose analogy, since its implicit scope is wider than lexical). Resolution is at elaboration to one
`../in/…` or `../out/…` binding, no handler search. The requirement is part of the head line, so it
is in the interface hash and a new requirement cascades; the explicit form stays for the escape
case (returning the capability into data). The effect line has two kinds of `+`: **capability
requirements** (`+in/net`, `+out/log`, resolved lexically) and **computation effects** (`+div`,
`+fail`; not values, never resolved).

## 2 Input and output are two capabilities, never one `io`

The merged `io` is a Unix file-descriptor and Haskell-`IO` habit, not a principle. Split per
channel into `in` (a source: a computation yielding a value, `F A`, governed by the integrity
polarity of the information-flow grade: how far is what comes in trusted) and `out` (a sink: a
computation consuming a value, `A → F 1`, governed by confidentiality: what may leave here). Grades
differ: a writer is unique, a reader may be shared, so reader/writer discipline is grades on two
capabilities. CHERI's read/write permission bits and Plan 9's open modes already split them. A
duplex channel is two capabilities; a session holds both in different states. `in` and `out` are
permission bits on a segment capability, not operations ([history and store](02.04.10-history-store-distribution.md)).

An observation to an adversary is an `out` sink too: `+out/obs/M` with a handler interpreting
`leak-branch`, `leak-address`, `tick` ([assurance](02.04.12-compiler-backends-assurance.md)).

## 3 Purity by absence: two computation effects

Effects moved from rows on arrows (Koka's `<io>`) to capability arguments. A definition with no
capability parameter is pure, a syntactic fact, not an analysis; the core's equational theory stays
that of pure CBPV. Exactly two effects remain on the computation type, because they are properties
of a computation's shape, not authority anyone holds:

- `div`: possible non-termination, where the totality checker cannot see productivity;
- `Fail`: escalation with `/`, which any frame may do without a capability, since giving up is not power.

Koka's `exn`, `div`, `ndet`, `st`, `io` become `Fail`, `div`, a randomness/scheduler capability, a
reference capability and two capabilities (`in`, `out`). The default fragment is total, pure and
non-failing; every departure is a word on the effect line: `+div`, `+fail`, or a capability.
Consequences: the e-graph-under-effects problem shrinks to a local rule (rewriting is valid
wherever no capability is in scope or applied, and those sites are visible as arguments); `once`
and hash memoisation are safe for any capability-free definition; staging may reorder and fuse
capability-free code freely. `div` did not dissolve in the worked example: a scan over codata is
productive by construction, and `div` remains for `lookup !` on non-literal quotations and for
recursion the checker cannot see ([totality](02.04.11-contracts-cost-totality.md)).

## 4 Failure, restarts and supervision

**Condition system.** Common Lisp's condition system and Erlang's supervisor trees on one
mechanism: effect handlers (Plotkin–Pretnar) are the condition system, because a handler receives
the continuation and may resume, and that is a restart. A failing frame *offers* restarts as
quotations closed over its own scope and escalates with `/`; the supervisor (the creating scope,
`..`) *chooses* one by forcing it, or escalates further. `⍣` (try) is a local supervisor. Every
escalation is a logged event, so restart-with-state is replay from the child's last checkpoint.

**The bare `/`.** `./` is self (`./x` bound here, `./norm` a send to self, open recursion free);
`../` is the enclosing scope, both the source of this scope's capabilities and its **supervisor**
(the creation chain, as in Erlang's spawn links, not the call stack); a bare `/` is **escalation**:
the frame gives up here and hands its failure up the `..` chain, with any restarts it offers pushed
first (`[ retry ] [ skip ] /`). The root is never readable (no spelling for reading it) but is
reachable by escalation: the root handlers are also the root supervisors, and a failure reaching
them with no restart chosen is the only form of panic.

**Checked failure without Java's failure (`+fail`).** Java's checked exceptions failed for three
reasons none intrinsic to checking: `throws` was nominal and not effect-polymorphic, nothing was
inferred, and catching was the only recovery. Koka's `exn` is the existence proof that checked
failure needs no boilerplate. Here a word carries `+fail` iff a path in it escalates or calls a
`+fail` word: inferred, shown on request, never written; effect variables carry it through
higher-order words (`map : xs [ x -- y +E ] -- ys +E`). *Caught* is structural: the `..` chain ends
at the root handlers, so "uncaught" cannot occur. *Where* it is caught is a static query on the
creation chain, and an operator may make it policy as a lint on the authority graph (no `+fail`
reaches the root except through a named supervisor). *Caught well* is exhaustiveness both ways:
the child's offered restarts are in its type (`+fail(retry skip)`), and a supervisor agenda that
does not answer an offered restart is a hole. Condition kinds are paths (`+fail ../fs/missing`),
matched by `match` on the path or structurally by `has/`; no class hierarchy. Failure as a *value*
(`Option`/`Result`) coexists for outcomes the caller can decide; escalation is for conditions only
a supervisor can decide. Rust's `?` is manual escalation without restarts and is not needed. A new
failure path in a body changes the inferred head line and cascades to callers, which is correct and
where an operator may choose a `sorry` ([principles](02.04.07-principles-and-privileges.md), P2).
Class 2 concurrent subtrees are `+fail`-free or merge their failures deterministically
([concurrency](02.04.14-concurrency-logic-search.md)).

**Eiffel's `rescue`/`retry`.** Eiffel recovered without a supervisor: a routine's `rescue` clause
catches what fails in its body, then either `retry`s from the start after adjusting state or
finishes and fails to the caller. Taken: (1) the invariant of the object directory must hold at the
moment of escalation (a contract on `/`, checkable), beside the rule that linear resources are
released or offered before `/`; (2) self-retry needs no parent: a word that wraps its own body in
`⍣` is its own parent, so `rescue`/`retry` is a `⍣` clause of the word with the fixed place of
`require` and `ensure` ([contracts](02.04.11-contracts-cost-totality.md)); (3) failure is contract
failure, not control flow, the `Fail`/`Result` split confirmed. Where this design has more: the
failing frame can offer restarts upward.

**Bare `/` is `panic!`, typed.** In shape it is Rust's `panic!`, with four differences, each an
existing property. (1) It is in the type: `+fail` is inferred, so "this word cannot fail" is a
static fact, and the hard-real-time fragment requires `+fail`-freedom. (2) No unwinding: every
linear obligation is discharged before `/` statically, so nothing runs implicitly on the way up.
(3) Not unrecoverable: the supervisor catches it per frame and may restart the child with state from
the log (let-it-crash at frame granularity). (4) The backtrace is the log: provenance is the
replayable events that led to the frame. Panic sites such as an out-of-bounds index are contract
violations here, impossible in proved code and escalating with blame at holes. The only
unrecoverable panic is the root with no restart chosen: the image aborts and restarts from
checkpoint plus replay ([history](02.04.10-history-store-distribution.md)). The cost, stated: `+fail`
appears in the head line and cascades; under P2 it is dispensable (`../adyton/unchecked`, `+nefas`),
and `unwrap()` is available with a name in the register instead of silence in the code.

## 5 Dynamic scope as a declared requirement: two trees

Jai's `context` with `push_context`, Emacs Lisp's `let`-rebinding of a global and Stallman's
argument for dynamic scope are one thing: the *reader* effect (`ask`), of which dynamic scope is the
canonical instance. `push_context` is installing a handler around a call, permitted only to whoever
holds the capability to install it; the consuming code declares it on the effect line as
`+ctx/allocator`, a requirement resolved *dynamically* along the call stack instead of lexically
along `..`. There are two trees, the creation tree (`..`, directories) and the call tree
(`/proc/self/stack`); a requirement says which it climbs, and paths run between them (the
rhizome without abandoning either tree). The installer needs the capability, the consumer declares
the need; overriding behaviour from outside the creation tree is advice, installable only by a
holder of the send ([objects](02.04.09-objects-dispatch-visibility.md)). It is needed less often
than in Jai because `../alloc` is the parent's allocator. Costs: a dynamic requirement appears in
the authority graph as an "installed dynamically" node per call path; performance equals Jai's (a
handler known at staging specialises to an implicit pointer).

## 6 The authority graph

Because capabilities are values passed lexically, the elaborator computes for every definition the
set of capabilities it can reach (capture sets, as in Scala 3; Effekt's effect types; Joe-E as the
precedent for static reviewability of authority). The **authority graph**, which definition can
reach which capability along which paths, is published beside the residual: content-addressed,
diffable, inspected on every change. Level-0 grants (`bind` at run time) are the only dynamic part
and need their own capability; an operator who withholds it has a fully static authority graph.
The IFC grade makes forbidden flows static (noninterference); a facet type may name the identities
it may be granted to, restoring "only X may call this" for identities rather than classes.
Confinement theorem owed (R9): the static graph bounds every possible log. The authority graph is a
done-when of B0 and B3 ([work graph](02.04.04-substrate.md)); profiles and speculation lints
attach to it ([principles](02.04.07-principles-and-privileges.md),
[assurance](02.04.12-compiler-backends-assurance.md)). Sealing means mentioning a sealed capability
yields an opaque node, never its contents.
